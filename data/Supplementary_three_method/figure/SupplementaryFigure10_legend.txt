Supplementary Figure 10. Reproducibility of gene-sleep-phenotype associations across
three tree-reconstruction criteria.

For each of the 143 circadian and sleep-related genes, gene trees were reconstructed
independently under three optimality criteria - neighbour joining on T92 distances (NJ),
maximum parsimony (MP; phangorn, parsimony ratchet, 2,000 bootstrap replicates) and
maximum likelihood (ML; IQ-TREE, ModelFinder Plus, 1,000 ultrafast-bootstrap and 1,000
SH-aLRT replicates) - from the same MUSCLE alignments and the same phenotype-specific
species sets. All downstream steps (average-linkage clustering of patristic distances,
cluster number and the association test) were held identical, so that the inferred
topology is the only quantity that differs. Criteria are ordered NJ, MP, ML throughout
this figure and in all accompanying supplementary tables.

Significance is defined separately by trait type and this definition is used
consistently in every panel. For the two continuous traits (NREM ratio, sleep duration),
cluster membership was tested by one-way ANOVA and genes were called significant at a
Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P below 0.05. For the two categorical traits (sleep timing,
sleep frequency), a 2 x K contingency table was tested by Fisher's exact test calibrated
by 20,000 permutations at fixed K, and genes were called significant at a raw
permutation P below 0.05, following the convention of the primary analysis. Adjusted
values for the categorical traits are provided in the source tables.

(A) Number of genes reaching significance under each criterion, for each phenotype
(143 genes tested per phenotype).

(B) Distribution of all 143 genes per phenotype by the number of criteria under which
they are significant (3/3, 2/3, 1/3, 0/3). Counts of fewer than ten genes are drawn
outside the bar with a leader line; zero counts are omitted.

(C) Spearman rank correlation of -log10(P) between each pair of criteria, computed over
all 143 genes and therefore independent of any significance threshold. Note that P
values index statistical evidence rather than effect size, so this panel measures
concordance of the ranking of gene-level association statistics, not of effect
magnitudes.

(D) Significance calls and association statistics for eight representative candidate
genes, each shown for the phenotype in which it was prioritised. Values indicate
BH-adjusted P values for continuous traits and raw permutation P values for categorical
traits; shaded tiles denote a value below 0.05 under the criterion indicated.

Note that for NREM ratio and sleep duration nearly every gene is significant under every
criterion, so that the minimum overlap attainable from the observed set sizes already
approaches the observed overlap; the high retention rates for those two phenotypes are
therefore structurally constrained and the rank concordances in (C) are the more
informative summary.
